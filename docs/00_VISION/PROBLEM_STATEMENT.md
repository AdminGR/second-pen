# Problem Statement

Source: [Blueprint](../BRAINSTORMING/Second-Pen-BLUEPRINT.md).

## Current Problem

AI writing tools generate from a blank context every session. A person who produces social copy, web copy, scripts, and documentation gets a different author each time. The outputs do not accumulate into a voice. They accumulate into a pile of almost-right text that has to be rewritten into the person's actual cadence, vocabulary, and restraint.

The gap is not "more templates" or "a better prompt." It is the absence of a persistent author: someone — or something — that already knows how this person writes, and can apply that across destinations.

## Why It Matters

When the seams show, the work is not shippable without another pass. That pass is the job a professional in-house copywriter does: they carry a brand's or a person's voice across every deliverable so a landing page, a caption, and a support doc sound like one writer. People without that role on staff, or who are that role, currently re-teach the model every time.

A meaningful share of the writing is meant to be spoken (Reels, scripts). Copy that scans on the page can still fail in the ear. Tools that only offer a read-and-edit path miss that failure.

## Existing Alternatives

- Generic chat models with a "write like me" instruction. No durable memory. Voice collapses between sessions.
- Template and calendar tools. They organize output. They do not author it in one voice.
- Brand-guideline documents pasted into prompts. Fragile, unread, and not destination-aware (a Reel and a manual step are not paced the same way).
- Human copywriters. They solve the problem. They do not scale to every caption and every manual step.

## Opportunity

Externalize the copywriter role as a tool that is legible as one job: brief in, voice-shaped draft out, review by eye or ear, push to the place it will be used. Keep the product that small on purpose. The voice model underneath can be sophisticated; the surface must not become a writing OS.
