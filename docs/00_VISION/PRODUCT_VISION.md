# Product Vision

Source: [Blueprint](../BRAINSTORMING/Second-Pen-BLUEPRINT.md). Engine notes stay in brainstorming until architecture is promoted.

## Vision

Second Pen is a persistent author a person can call on for any piece of writing, across any medium, and get their own voice back. It is the in-house copywriter role, externalized into a tool: one voice carried across a Reel script, a paragraph of web copy, a manual step, and a video script, so the seams do not show.

It is not a content generator with a template picker, a dashboard, or a library. It is one continuous action: take an input (spoken or typed), run it through a model of how this specific person writes, shape the result for where it is going, and hand back a draft that can be reviewed by eye or by ear before it ships.

## User

A person who writes across more than one medium and needs the output to sound like it came from the same author. Primary use is expected to be mobile and voice-first. Desktop is the same product on a larger surface, not a separate product.

"Second Pen" is a working name, not a confirmed brand.

## Problem

Every generic AI writing session starts from zero. Social copy on Monday and a manual paragraph on Thursday do not read like the same person wrote them, because the model has no memory of how this person writes between sessions. A professional copywriter carries that voice in their head. Most tools do not.

## Desired Outcome

A person can dictate or type a brief, name a destination, get a draft in their voice shaped for that destination's mechanics, review it by reading or listening, and push it forward into wherever it will actually be used. Stack twenty of those outputs and they still sound like one author.

## Principles

- One job, done well. Medium is a parameter on that job, not a mode or a screen.
- Light, fast, obvious, singular. Obvious within a second. No onboarding required.
- Type and dictate are equals. Read and listen are equals. A share of this copy is written to be spoken.
- The draft is a document, not a chat turn. It pins, edits, and versions.
- History, libraries, and settings are quiet secondary affordances, not the product.
- Latency is a product requirement.
- Voice is seeded quietly and improved from what the person actually accepts. The library is not the product.
- Architecture stays behind the surface. Authentic voice, influence, and facts must never become one soup — that is an engine rule, not a screen.
